Did the regularised and crop150 arms converge?

Generated 2026-09-15 15:03 CST by scripts/digests/build_convergence_curves.py from local run artifacts. Evidence: digest/convergence-audit.json.

Two results were recorded without looking at the shape of the training curve: the stochastic-depth sweep and the 150 mm crop re-baseline. Both were read as “this lever costs retrieval”. Neither reading could separate two different explanations:

  1. the lever makes the representation worse; or
  2. the lever slows optimisation, and a fixed 20-epoch budget stopped the run before it finished improving.

The epoch histories already on disk distinguish them, and they give different answers for the two levers.

Answer, in one line each. Drop-path: the hypothesis holds — at rate 0.1 the run is still sitting at its own validation minimum when the budget ends (rise from minimum +0.010, against the baseline’s +0.259), with the tail slope still negative in 3 of 3 seeds. Those arms had not finished improving, so the sweep compared rates at unequal degrees of completion. crop150: the hypothesis fails — it reached its validation minimum earlier than the baseline (epoch 9.0 vs 11.3) and then overfit at least as hard, so its lower retrieval is not a truncation artifact.

The fourth column is the load-bearing one, not the fifth. “Still falling at epoch 20” is a two-point sign test on tail differences of roughly 0.001–0.015 — at the low rates that is noise around zero, so its 1/3, 0/3, 1/3, 1/3 counts should not be read as a dose-response. The rise from each arm’s own minimum is monotone across all six rates and is the column the finding rests on.

Read the fourth column down the drop-path rows. The validation-loss rise collapses monotonically — +0.259 → +0.184 → +0.125 → +0.102 → +0.057 → +0.010 — and from rate 0.01 upward the validation minimum moves steadily later, 10.3 → 14.0 → 14.0 → 16.0 at rate 0.1. (The two lowest rates sit at 10.3, marginally earlier than the baseline’s 11.3, so the minimum-epoch column is monotone only over the upper four rates; the rise column is monotone over all six.) The regulariser is doing exactly what a regulariser does: it delays the onset of overfitting and it slows optimisation. At rate 0.1 the run has barely begun to overfit when the budget ends.

This is a confound in the sweep, not a correction to its arithmetic. Every rate was trained for the same 20 epochs, but the higher rates need more epochs to reach their own optimum. So the sweep did not compare six rates at convergence — it compared six rates at progressively earlier points along their own trajectories. The measured retrieval deficit at high rates is therefore a mixture of a genuine effect and a truncation artifact, and these runs cannot say how much of it is which.

Training loss — drop-path arms

Do not compare these levels across arms, and do not read the distance to the validation panel as a generalisation gap. Two separate reasons, both of which have produced a wrong claim in this project before:
  • train_loss is computed in-batch over 32 with augmentation on, in train mode (training.py:158). validation_loss is the full 441-candidate matrix, unaugmented, in eval mode (evaluation.py:200). They are different quantities on different denominators.
  • Drop-path is active in the arm’s train-mode forward pass and identity in the baseline’s, so any forward-pass noise raises the arm’s train loss regardless of what it memorised. The higher final train loss at higher rates is partly this artifact and partly real slower optimisation.
The curve’s shape is readable; its level against another arm is not. The admissible memorisation route is the matched-N eval-mode gap in drop-path-sweep-v1.md.

Validation loss — drop-path arms

This is the panel that answers the question. The baseline turns upward around epoch 11 and climbs for the rest of the run. Each successive rate turns later and more shallowly, and rate 0.1 has essentially not turned at all by epoch 20.

Part of the vertical separation here is temperature, not representation quality. The learned τ⁻¹ drifts downward with rate, toward the validation-optimal value of 11.50 — and rescaling τ at evaluation time changes retrieval by exactly zero. So a lower validation-loss curve is not by itself a better representation. The crop150 re-baseline is a different story

These curves share no axis with the drop-path panels above. crop_mm 130 → 150 changes the pixels, so the crop150 runs are not comparable to any crop130 run. They are drawn beside the baseline only to compare curve shape, which is a statement about optimisation, not about score.

crop150 converged and then overfit; it was not truncated. Its validation minimum arrives at epoch 9.0 against the baseline’s 11.3 — earlier, not later — and it then drifts up +0.280, slightly more than the baseline’s +0.259. Its best epoch is at the boundary in 0 of 3 seeds. Every signature points the same way: the 150 mm crop is a harder optimisation problem that the model finishes early and then memorises. The user’s hypothesis does not rescue this result, and crop150-rebaseline-v1.md’s reading stands.

Would more epochs actually help? The one run that bears on it

image-block-epochs-v1/original-40ep-seed2026 is the project's only run at a budget other than 20 epochs. Its config-exact twin at 20 epochs is image-block-cohort-compare-v1/original-multilingual-seed2026 — diffed field by field, zero differences apart from epochs.

Doubling the budget improved retrieval by +0.0079 — while validation loss ran away from 3.687 to 4.752. That is a large divergence in the loss and a small gain in the selection metric, in the same run.

This is the single most important caveat on this page, and it cuts against the diagnostic the rest of the page is built on. A rising validation loss does not imply that more epochs cannot help retrieval — here the loss nearly ran away and R@1 still improved for another 14 epochs. It is another instance of the dissociation in val-loss-vs-retrieval-audit.md. So the convergence diagnostics above establish that the high-rate drop-path arms were still improving when the budget ended; they do not by themselves predict how much retrieval a longer budget would buy. Only a budget arm measures that, and it must be decided on R@1, not on validation loss.
Three reasons this run cannot settle the question on its own. It adapts 1 block, not 4, so it is not a comparator for any arm on this page — it is excluded from every panel and from the diagnostic table for that reason. The cosine schedule is stretched over the full 40 epochs, so this is a different learning-rate trajectory throughout rather than the 20-epoch run continued. And it is one seed, against a seed spread of 0.023.

What this page deliberately does not show

Per-class accuracy and AUROC cannot be curves here, and for the drop-path and crop150 arms they do not exist even as points.
  • AUROC comes from evaluate_downstream_probe.py run against a saved checkpoint, and training keeps only best.pt and last.pt. There are no per-epoch checkpoints to probe, so an AUROC-versus-epoch curve would require retraining with per-epoch saves. build_extended_curves.py records the same limitation for the arms it covers.
  • No downstream probe has ever been run on the drop-path or crop150 checkpoints. The sweep’s probe box is unchecked by decision (selection is retrieval R@1, and the arm lost on it), and the crop150 probe is flagged in the handoff as the first thing to run with a human present because it needs a hand-built config. So there is no macro AUROC, no per-class AUROC and no per-class accuracy for these arms at all — not a missing curve, a missing measurement.
  • In place of “accuracy” this page reports Recall@1, which is the project’s actual selection metric and is logged every epoch.
A probe pass on the crop150 checkpoints would produce the macro and per-class AUROC, and is the obvious next step — but it is a user decision, not something to launch unattended.

Follow-up: the budget arm ran, and it half-confirms this page

Both arms retrained at 40 epochs, 3 seeds (budget-40ep-v1.md). Both converged inside the new budget (best epochs 23–35 of 40), so the comparison is now at convergence rather than at a truncation. Drop-path 0.1 gained +0.0150 from the longer budget against the baseline’s +0.0062 — the asymmetry this page predicted — and the deficit fell from −0.0255 to −0.0167, its interval moving from [−0.0421, −0.0094] to [−0.0337, +0.0005], which now grazes zero instead of excluding it. But the deficit did not vanish, and the pre-registered rule returns ambiguous. Truncation was real and worth about a third of the measured harm; it was not the whole of it.
The caveat above was the right one. This page warned that its diagnostics establish only that the high-rate arms were still improving — not how much a longer budget would buy — because the 40-epoch precedent improved retrieval while validation loss ran away. That held: the arms were genuinely truncated, and the recovery was real but partial.

What follows from this

  1. “Stochastic depth is closed at 4 blocks” is too strong — and so is “it was all truncation”. The budget arm settled this in part: a third of the measured harm was budget, and a directional deficit survives at convergence without excluding zero. Both clean stories fail, which is the honest result.
  2. A fixed epoch budget is not a neutral choice when the lever is a regulariser. Projection-head dropout and random erasing are both still untested; either must train to a convergence criterion or report budget as a co-varying factor.
  3. The recorded 0.1340 baseline is a 20-epoch number, not a ceiling — it gained +0.0062 at 40 epochs. Anything comparing against it should say which budget it means.
  4. The “every lever that removes information fails the same way” through-line survives, but weakened on one leg. The crop leg is unaffected — crop150 converged. The stochastic-depth leg is now partly confounded with budget.
  5. Selection stays retrieval R@1. Nothing here was selected on label AUROC, and nothing on this page changes that.
