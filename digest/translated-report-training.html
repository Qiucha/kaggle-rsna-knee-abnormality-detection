BiomedBERT leads across five folds

Frozen BiomedBERT reached 19.77 ± 6.59% bidirectional mean Recall@1, compared with 17.20 ± 8.92% for Stanford RadBERT. This reverses the original single-split ordering and confirms that one 12-study holdout was not stable enough for model selection. Unfreezing the final one or two blocks improved single-holdout mean R@1 from 16.67% to 20.83%; extending inputs from 256 to 512 tokens reduced it to 12.50%.

Baseline-compatible image path

  1. Prefer fluid-sensitive series; choose the largest per plane.
  2. Geometrically order slices and sample three over the central 20–80%.
  3. Apply a centered 130 mm crop when pixel spacing permits.
  4. Clip at series percentiles 1–99, scale to [0,1], resize to 224.
  5. Normalize right knees to the left-knee convention; augment without random flips.

BiomedBERT is the more stable candidate. Its bidirectional mean R@1 is 19.77 ± 6.59%, versus 17.20 ± 8.92% for Stanford RadBERT. The fold spread remains large relative to the difference between models, so this is a candidate-selection result rather than a definitive performance estimate.

Report-length ablation

The frozen BiomedBERT control isolates maximum input length. BiomedBERT tokenization puts 24/58 reports above 256 tokens and 5/58 above 512; median length is 236.5, p90 is 441, and maximum is 710 tokens.

At each run's best epoch, 512 tokens raises mean R@5 from 54.17% to 58.33%, but lowers the primary mean R@1 from 16.67% to 12.50%. More context is not automatically better on this split; section-aware findings/impression extraction is the cleaner next test.

Interpretation

The newest evidence favors frozen BiomedBERT for model selection and cautious two-block adaptation as a follow-up. Cross-validation overturned Stanford RadBERT's original single-split lead. The adaptation and token-length results remain exploratory because they use only one holdout and select the best epoch on that same validation set.

Retrieval measures pair identification, not diagnostic correctness. With only 11–12 candidates per fold, one match moves fold R@1 by 8.3–9.1 percentage points, and patient-disjointness still cannot be established from the available tables.
